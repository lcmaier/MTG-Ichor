An Exception Is Judged Without Itself

CV-2b lets a permanent that enters as a copy make CR 707.9's exceptions. 707.9a–d change the copied values, once, as they are captured. 707.9e adds a status or counters to the entry. 707.9f makes an exception apply only if the copy is something, and the copy it asks about has not been made yet. So CV-2b adds one new kind of read: a CR 614.12 look-ahead of a hypothetical copy, taken once per judgment, inside the CR 616.1c application. Here are three boards, walked read by read from the records the engine's trace sink wrote.

The map

Spark Double's text is three exceptions: "an additional +1/+1 counter on it if it's a creature" (call it the creature exception), "an additional loyalty counter on it if it's a planeswalker" (the planeswalker exception), and "it isn't legendary". The first two are 707.9e counters under 707.9f conditions. The third is a 707.9b edit, and a copy of the copy keeps it.

707.9f says to judge each conditional exception against "the resulting permanent's characteristics ... if the copy effect were applied without that exception, taking into account any other exceptions that effect includes". Trace A is the ordinary case. Trace B is the board where the two conditional exceptions decide each other's condition. Trace C is 707.9e's other half: a later copy in the same entry takes an exception back.

Trace A — Spark Double copies a legend

The ordinary case, and the shape every copy with exceptions follows. The record numbers are the trace's seq.

  1. change_zone(Spark Double, Battlefield, Returned) proposes EnterBattlefield { mods: EnterMods::NONE } state (record 2). Iteration 1 builds the CR 614.12 frame of Spark Double itself, a 0/0 Illusion (record 4), and source 1a finds its own copy ability: one candidate, on CR 616.1c's step.
  2. entry_copy_donor asks player 0 to choose among "a creature or planeswalker you control": Isamaru alone, under "you may" (record 6) player. copiable_values(Isamaru) captures the values after layer 1: a legendary 2/2 creature.
  3. enter_as_copy: there is no earlier copy to take back. Judge::applying(0) judges each conditional exception without itself, in printed order. The creature exception first. Inside its judgment the planeswalker exception is judged without both, on the copy with only "isn't legendary": a 2/2 creature and no planeswalker (record 7) frame, so it does not apply there, and the creature exception's own frame is that same copy (record 8): a creature, so it applies. Then the planeswalker exception. Inside its judgment the creature exception, judged without both, applies (record 9, the same frame again), so the planeswalker exception's own frame carries that counter: a 3/3 creature and no planeswalker (record 10), so it does not apply.
  4. Judge::outcome makes the exceptions that apply. CopiableValues::except removes Legendary from the captured supertypes, through TypeChange::modifications, the lowering Primitive::ChangeType uses. evaluate_enter_template turns "an additional +1/+1 counter" into one row. strip_prohibited_counters refuses nothing, and EnterMods::merge puts the row on the entry, which EntryCopy.added records state.
  5. Iteration 2's frame is the copy with its counter, a 3/3 (record 12). Nothing is gathered (record 13), and the performer places a nonlegendary Isamaru with one +1/+1 counter (records 14–15). The copy's values reach the board pass at layer 1a as a finished snapshot, as CV-2a's did.
Four look-aheads for two conditions. Each conditional exception is judged without itself, and inside that, the other is judged without both. The judgments are memoized on what is left out, so a subset the recursion reaches twice is judged once. The frames are not, so records 7, 8 and 9 are one frame taken three times. It happens only for a copy with conditional exceptions: Spark Double, and Moritte of the Frost when it is built.

Trace B — Spark Double copies Kaito, beside Oath of Gideon

Kaito, Bane of Nightmares: "During your turn, as long as Kaito has one or more loyalty counters on him, he's a 3/4 Ninja creature". While he is a creature he is not a planeswalker (his seventh ruling). With 4 loyalty on him he is a creature, so Spark Double may choose him.

  1. The donor question offers Kaito alone (record 6) player. Oath's filter reads Spark Double's frame, a 0/0 creature and no planeswalker, so Oath is not a candidate at iteration 1 (record 11: one candidate).
  2. The creature exception first, judged without itself. Inside that, the planeswalker exception is judged without both, on the copy with only "isn't legendary" and no counters. Kaito's condition reads no loyalty counter, so the frame is a planeswalker (record 7) frame, and in that hypothetical the planeswalker exception applies.
  3. So the creature exception's own frame carries that loyalty counter. Kaito's condition holds, and it is a 3/4 creature (record 8) frame. The creature exception applies.
  4. Then the planeswalker exception, judged without itself. Inside that, the creature exception judged without both sees the same planeswalker (record 9), so it does not apply there, and the planeswalker exception's own frame is that copy with no counters (record 10): a planeswalker, so it applies too.
  5. Both counters go on the entry (record 13's event: one +1/+1 and one loyalty). Iteration 2's frame is a 4/5 creature (record 12). It is no planeswalker, so the walk does not synthesize CR 306.5b's ability on it, and Oath's "planeswalkers you control" does not match. Nothing is gathered, and nothing is asked but the donor.
The gray area this board sits on. Two register rows (engineering-practices.md §3.4b) meet here. lookahead-entry-counters: the look-ahead counts the counters an entry already carries, by CR 614.12's text. Arixmethes's ruling reads it otherwise, and so does Spark Double's ruling #7 ("printed on the card plus one"), which gives 5 here; the engine gives 1, and the ruling keeps a // RULING-DEVIATION: test. copy-exception-conditions: 707.9f does not say how two conditional exceptions read each other. Its words, taken literally, give both counters, and they are the only reading that passes its own test here: the other one gives the loyalty counter and then, judging the creature exception, sees a creature. WotC has been asked this board twice and has not answered (§4.1a's evidence). Each row's switch is one place in the code: the SourceHasCounters arm, and the mask Judge::applying passes to matches.

Trace C — a later copy takes the counter back

CR 707.9e: "If another copy effect is applied to that object after applying the copy effect with that exception, the exception's effect doesn't happen." Its example is Altered Ego copying a Clone that copied nothing. Here it is Spark Double.

  1. The donor question offers the empty Clone alone, the one creature player 0 controls (record 6) player. The judgments (records 7–10) find a creature: the creature exception applies, and the entry carries one +1/+1 counter (record 12: a 2/2 under the Anthem).
  2. Iteration 2: the copied Clone ability is on the frame, so source 1a gathers it, another CR 616.1c effect (record 14's candidate). Its donor question offers the Bears and the empty Clone (record 13) player, and the Bears are chosen.
  3. enter_as_copy finds an earlier copy on the entry, and EnterMods::take_back subtracts what its added recorded: the one +1/+1 counter state. Clone has no exceptions, so the Bears' values go on as they were captured.
  4. Iteration 3's frame is a 3/3 Grizzly Bears, the Anthem's 2/2 with no counter (record 15). Nothing is gathered (record 16), and it enters so.
A status is taken back too, and not over a later one. An exception may set the entry's status ("except it enters untapped", read as 707.9e). A status is set, not added, so added keeps the one it replaced, and EnterMods::merge drops that claim once a later effect sets a status: a take-back then leaves the later status alone. Counters are exact in CR 616.1's order. Item 189 records the one inexact case, a multiplier between two copies, which no registered card reaches.

What CV-2b changed, trace by trace, and what is still open

A is the shape. The values are made once, at the capture, so layer 1a applies a finished snapshot and compute.rs did not change. The entry's own writes live on the entry, beside the record of what they were.

B is the finding. 707.9f's condition is read against a copy that does not exist yet, and where a copy's counter changes what the copy is, the two conditions read each other. The recursion is the words of the rule, and the gray area is two register rows, each one switch.

C is 707.9e's other half: a later copy undoes exactly what an earlier one's exceptions added.

Open, each with its owner. Item 189, the take-back beside a multiplier, is slotted with the feedback-loop census (roadmap-v2.md A6i). The facilities §7c lists for the other printed exceptions (a donor off the battlefield, an exception that reads the donor, X, changeling, 707.9g's linked trigger) land with their cards.
