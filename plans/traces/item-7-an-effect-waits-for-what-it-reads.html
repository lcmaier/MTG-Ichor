An Effect Waits For What It Reads

CR 613.7 orders a layer by timestamp; CR 613.8 overrides that whenever applying one effect would change what another reads — its existence, what it applies to, or what it does. LI-1 built the board the reads run against (its own page); this one is the two phases that close item 7. LI-2 made the order a decision taken against the live board and re-taken after every application. LI-3 added one more thing an effect's existence can depend on — a condition — and with it a read the static check has to know about, or a whole class of pairs is settled "independent" by mistake. Four boards, walked one call at a time: the four-card layer 4 a judge's answer walks, the board where a condition is the dependency, the board where a condition is not one and it matters that the engine can tell, and the one board here whose order needs CR 613.8c rather than just its sets.

The map

One loop per layer, and the loop's first line is a decision. applications_in_layer collects what the layer applies and sorts it on CR 613.3's key; resolve_order_within_layer then applies exactly one thing per iteration, asking next_ready which one. That is where CR 613.8 lives, and it is the whole of the phase — perform below it is LI-1's, unchanged.

next_ready has a fast path and a slow one. The fast path asks only whether the key-first application waits on anything; if not, it is next by construction and no graph is built. The slow path builds the pairwise matrix, closes it transitively, and takes the earliest member of a source component — which is CR 613.8b's loop rule and CR 613.8a's waiting rule in one line. Each pair goes to depends_on, which tries the static channel check first and only then clones the board under a Journal; the dotted edges are that answer coming back to whoever asked for it.

What an application is, and why it is not a row

The unit CR 613.8 orders is an effect, and every clause of the rule says so. That is not a naming detail. Ashaya's "Nontoken creatures you control are Forest lands in addition to their other types" lowers to two layer-4 registry rows — AddType(Land) and AddSubtype(Forest) — and if those were ordered separately Blood Moon could apply between them: after the first row the creatures are nonbasic lands, Blood Moon depends on nothing still pending, and the second row then paints Forest onto lands Blood Moon has just made Mountains, through the CR 613.6 set it locked. Kind::Effect bundles one EffectGroup's rows in a layer and applies them as one thing.

A · Four effects, one layer

The board above, plus a basic Forest and a Grizzly Bears, entered in the reverse of the order they apply in: Urborg first, then Blood Moon, then Ashaya, then Opalescence. Timestamp order would apply Urborg first and paint Swamp onto everything. What the CR gives instead is Opalescence → Ashaya → Blood Moon, with Urborg never applying at all — and the sequence is not computed once. Each of the four steps below re-runs next_ready against the board the previous step left, which is CR 613.8c.

Asserted step by step in board.rs's the_four_card_board_applies_opalescence_ashaya_blood_moon_and_urborg_never, through the pass's trace hook.

  1. A0applications_in_layer(game, board, Layer4Type, 3)board.rslive

    Four Kind::Effect applications, one per card, each carrying its Reads and Channels. Sorted on the key: no CDAs at layer 4, so timestamp order — Urborg, Blood Moon, Ashaya, Opalescence.

  2. A1next_ready(.., pending = [U, BM, Ash, Opa])board.rsthe fast path fails on the first rowLI-2

    Urborg is key-first, so the fast path asks only about it. depends_on(U, BM): Urborg's reads.source is ABILITIES (CR 604.2), Blood Moon writes SUBTYPES | ABILITIES | KEYWORDS (CR 305.7), and Blood Moon reaches Urborg — a Legendary Land is nonbasic. Overlap, so the check goes to the board.

  3. A2observe(U) · perform(BM, journal) · observe(U) · restoreboard.rsjournal

    Before: exists = true, affected = every land. After Blood Moon: Urborg is a Mountain, CR 305.7 stripped its ability, exists = false. The observations differ, so Urborg waits for Blood Moon. The journal restores the frames it touched and the board is exactly as it was.

  4. A3next_ready — the slow pathboard.rsthe whole matrix, then the closureLI-2

    Urborg waits, so the graph is built over all four — the matrix below. Opalescence applies — the one application in a source component. It reaches Blood Moon, making it a creature.

  5. A4next_ready(.., pending = [U, BM, Ash])board.rslive

    Re-decided against a board where Blood Moon is now a creature. Ashaya applies — and because Opalescence went first she reaches Blood Moon as well as herself and the Bears. Two rows, one application, so nothing lands between AddType(Land) and AddSubtype(Forest).

  6. A5next_ready(.., pending = [U, BM])board.rslive

    Urborg still waits on Blood Moon; Blood Moon waits on nothing left. Blood Moon applies — and it now reaches four objects, not one: Urborg, Ashaya, the Bears and itself, all nonbasic lands as of A4. CR 305.7 strips their abilities, Ashaya's own P/T CDA included.

  7. A6perform(U) → row_affected → static_ability_still_existsboard.rslive

    Affected::Gone. Urborg's frame no longer carries the ability that generates its effect, so it applies to nothing — "no matter in what order those effects started to apply", as its 2021-03-19 ruling puts it.

The honest reading of these four matrices: on this board CR 613.8c is not what decides the order. Round 1's graph, closed transitively, already gives Opalescence → Ashaya → Blood Moon → Urborg; rounds 2 to 4 re-derive the same relations minus the applied row and would be skippable. What re-evaluation changes here is what each application reaches — Ashaya reaches Blood Moon only because Opalescence went first, and Blood Moon reaches four objects only because Ashaya did — and that is a consequence of applying in order, not of re-deciding. The board where the order itself needs 613.8c is trace D, and it is a fixture rather than a printed pair. Worth saying, because the four-card board is the one the judge answer walks and it is easy to read its "re-evaluated after each effect is applied" as load-bearing for the sequence rather than for the sets.

The hypothetical counts are the engine's, not the matrix's size: 12 pairs in round 1 but 9 clones, because three pairs are settled by the channel check without touching the board. Those three are the ones where Blood Moon or Opalescence is asked whether it waits for something that writes a channel neither reads.

The judge's answer states two of this phase's decisions in its own words. "The dependency has to be actual for the current game state being built, not theoretical" is A2's journal applying against the live board rather than a hypothetical one; "re-evaluated after each effect is applied" is A4 and A5 re-running the whole decision. plans/references/blood-moon-urborg-ashaya-opalescence-judge-answer.md has it in full.

B · A condition as the dependency

LI-3's board. Simian Clause — a fixture, "As long as you control a Forest, each creature you control is an Ape in addition to its other types" — enters first, with a Taiga (a nonbasic Mountain Forest) and a Grizzly Bears; Blood Moon enters second. Both effects are layer 4. Under timestamp order the Clause applies while the Taiga is still a Forest and the Bears becomes an Ape. The CR says it does not, and the reason is the same as Urborg's: applying Blood Moon changes whether the Clause's effect exists.

What is new is where that existence question lives. Urborg's is an ability lookup — is the ability still on the frame. The Clause's ability is still there; what changed is the board its condition asks about. static_ability_still_exists answers both, and LI-3 is the second clause in it.

  1. B0effect_channels(game, board, 3, rows)board.rsLI-3

    The Clause is a static, so reads.source |= ABILITIES. Then conditional_reads_of finds Effect::Conditional on the source's live frame and adds the condition's channels: ControlPermanent(BySubtype(Forest)) reads CONTROLLER on every member (the variant's own controller test) and SUBTYPES through the filter, plus CONTROLLER on the source for CR 109.5's "you".

  2. B1depends_on(Clause, BM) — the static halfboard.rsno board

    on_source: ABILITIES | CONTROLLER against Blood Moon's SUBTYPES | ABILITIES | KEYWORDS — overlapping, but Blood Moon does not reach the Clause, an enchantment. on_members: TYPES | CONTROLLER | SUBTYPES against the same — SUBTYPES overlaps, and that bit is the condition's. The pair goes to the board.

  3. B1′the same call, without conditional_reads_ofthe sabotage runwrong

    on_members would be TYPES | CONTROLLER — the Clause's own filter, "creatures you control" — which is disjoint from what Blood Moon writes. on_source overlaps but Blood Moon reaches no Clause. Settled independent, no hypothetical, timestamp order, a Bears that is an Ape. Exactly one test of LI-3's six fails this way, and it is the one written for it.

  4. B2observe(Clause) → row_affected → static_ability_still_exists → condition::holdscondition.rsLI-3live

    controls_matching walks Board::battlefield_ids, asking each member's live frame. The Taiga's frame still carries Subtype::Land(Forest); its controller is "you". exists = true, affected = [Bears].

  5. B3perform(BM, journal) then observe(Clause) againboard.rsjournal

    Blood Moon sets the Taiga's subtypes to {Mountain}. condition::holds runs again against that frame and finds no Forest: exists = false, affected = []. The observations differ — the Clause depends on Blood Moon — and the journal puts the Taiga's frame back.

  6. B4next_ready returns Blood Moon; performboard.rslive

    Blood Moon applies for real, to the one nonbasic land. One hypothetical was run in the whole layer: the reverse pair is settled statically, since Blood Moon reads land types and supertypes and the Clause writes a creature subtype on creatures.

  7. B5perform(Clause) → Affected::Goneboard.rsLI-3

    By the Clause's turn there is no Forest, so its effect does not exist and it reaches nothing. The Bears is a Bear. Put a basic Forest on the board and B3's two observations agree — no dependency, timestamp order, and the Bears is an Ape after all, which is the control the test carries.

C · A condition that is not a dependency

Kird Ape, a Taiga and Blood Moon. "This creature gets +1/+2 as long as you control a Forest" is a layer 7c effect; Blood Moon is layer 4. The Ape loses its bonus, and it is important that the engine reaches that answer without CR 613.8 having anything to say — clause (a) confines dependency to a single layer, and two effects in different layers never meet in next_ready at all. The condition simply reads a board where layer 4 has already happened.

  1. C1layer 4: resolve_order_within_layer, one applicationboard.rsno board

    Blood Moon alone. next_ready short-circuits at pending.len() == 1 — no matrix, no hypothetical, no channel comparison. The Taiga's live frame becomes a Mountain.

  2. C2layers 5, 6, 7a, 7bboard.rsempty

    Nothing to apply. The frames carry layer 4's answer forward, which is the whole point of the pass being sequential over one map.

  3. C3layer 7c: row_affected(Ape's row) → condition::holdscondition.rsLI-3live

    ControlPermanent(BySubtype(Forest)) against the live frames: the Taiga is a Mountain and nothing else has the subtype. false → Affected::Gone. The Ape is 1/1, and Dependency checks is unchanged across the whole pass.

  4. C4the same board with a basic Forest addedthe testlive

    Blood Moon reaches only nonbasic lands, so the basic Forest's frame keeps its subtype, the condition holds at 7c, and the Ape is 2/3. One registry row throughout — the row never changed, only the answer to whether it exists.

Why B and C are both in this page. They are the same sentence of card text — "as long as you control a Forest" — under the same Blood Moon, and the CR treats them completely differently because of which layer the effect lands in. An engine that routed every condition through the dependency check would run a hypothetical on C's board and get the same answer more slowly; one that routed none would get B's board wrong. The layer index is what separates them, and it costs nothing to consult because next_ready only ever sees one layer's applications.

D · The board where re-evaluation decides the order

Trace A's order falls out of round 1's graph; this one does not. Three enchantments, all layer 4, over one Idol (a vanilla Artifact) — and the timestamps are deliberately A, C, B, so the key order and the answer disagree:

timestamp 1 · A
Artifacts Are Elves
Artifacts are Elves. (AddSubtype(Elf) over ByType(Artifact))
timestamp 2 · C
Goblins Are Creatures
Goblins are creatures. (AddType(Creature) over BySubtype(Goblin))
timestamp 3 · B
Elves Are Also Goblins
Elves are Goblins. (AddSubtype(Goblin) over BySubtype(Elf))

The Idol is an Artifact and nothing else. So at the start of the layer it is not an Elf, and it is not a Goblin — which means C depends on nothing yet: applying B would turn Elves into Goblins, and there are no Elves, so nothing C reads would move. The dependency C→B does not exist until A has applied. That is CR 613.8c's whole content, and this is what it looks like.

  1. D1next_ready(.., pending = [A, C, B])board.rsthe fast path succeedsLI-2

    A is key-first and waits on nothing — two channel comparisons and no graph. A applies, and the Idol becomes an Elf. The relations at this moment, had the whole matrix been built: B waits on A; C waits on nothing.

  2. D2next_ready(.., pending = [C, B])board.rsand now the fast path failsjournal

    C is key-first. depends_on(C, B) now says yes — the Idol is an Elf, so applying B would make it a Goblin, which changes what C applies to. An edge that did not exist one application ago. The slow path runs, B is the source component, B applies and the Idol becomes a Goblin.

  3. D3next_ready(.., pending = [C]) → perform(C)board.rslive

    C applies to a Goblin that exists because of the two applications before it. The Idol ends the layer an Elf Goblin creature — applied A, B, C, where the key order was A, C, B.

  4. D′the same board, ordered once at D1the counterfactualwrong

    Take round 1's graph as the answer: A and C are both independent, so after A the next by key is C — which applies to no Goblin and reaches nothing. Then B. The Idol is an Elf Goblin and not a creature. That is the failure CR 613.8c exists to prevent, and it is what LI-2's sabotage run reproduces when next_ready is forced to key order.

The engine's own work on this board is one hypothetical, in round 2 — measured, per round: 0, 1, 0. Round 1 costs none because the fast path only ever asks about A, and both of A's pairs are settled without touching the board: A reads types and B writes subtypes (disjoint channels), and C affects nothing at all yet, which depends_on short-circuits on before any clone. So the single most expensive thing the loop does happens exactly at the moment the new edge appears, and nowhere else.

Why this one is a fixture and trace A is printed. Trace A's four cards are real and its answer is a published judge's walkthrough, but its order does not need re-evaluation. This board's does, and no printed pair in the registry builds it. The shape to look for is a three-effect chain in one layer whose middle link is created by the first — the CR's own stock example, "+1/+1 for each Elf you control" beside "creatures are Elves" beside something that makes the Elves, is the nearest printed neighbourhood. Recorded as still owed in layers-architecture.md §13b; the rule is tested, the card is not.

Where the reads differ

Every read the dependency check makes, and what answers it. The column that matters is the third: a read of the source is a dependency only when the other application reaches that source, and a read of the members is a dependency whenever the other application reaches anything at all. Splitting them is what keeps Urborg's pair — where both applications touch abilities — from going to the hypothetical in both directions.

And the one that is not re-asked. CR 613.6 locks the set an effect first applied to, and row_affected consults Board::started before it consults either existence question — so a multi-layer conditional effect whose condition goes false between its layers keeps applying to the set it locked. effect_channels mirrors that: when the group is locked, neither the ability read nor the condition read is added at all, because neither can be a dependency any more.

What item 7 closed, and what it left

  1. The three boards the CR's own rulings walk are answered as the CR answers them, in both timestamp orders: Blood Moon with Urborg (2021-03-19), with the Rootpath Purifier clause (2022-10-14), and Humility with Opalescence (2009-10-01, 2006-02-01). Ashaya beside Blood Moon has no ruling and its expected answer is derived from the CR, which the test says on its face.
  2. "As long as [X]" is not a new kind of row. It lowers to the inner atom's rows and the condition stays on the ability, which the existence check was already fetching. That is what kept ContinuousEffect from growing a field and the registry from needing a reconciliation pass — the thing CLAUDE.md warns against, because deciding existence outside the layer walk needs an iteration cap and invents oscillation the CR does not have.
  3. The A/B says the engine change is inert until a conditional card is in the pool, and says it exactly: with the registry unchanged, LI-3's binary reproduces main byte for byte on both pools — every counter, three serial timing rounds, and the whole 40-game event stream. Effect::Conditional was an arm no registered ability's body reached.
  4. Left open, and now written down. A static ability that grants a static ability still registers no continuous effect: the derived rows have exactly one producer and it is a resolution. That is why this page's fixture is Rune of Flight's third line and not its fourth, and it is codebase-state.md "Before Layers" item 7g.
  5. What item 6 inherits. Condition now has a static-context evaluator; CR 603.4's intervening "if" is the same AST read at a different moment, so the trigger phase adds a reader rather than a language. A new leaf is three edits, and the third is the one to remember: a leaf that reads a frame and does not say so in condition_reads produces a wrong order, not a wrong value, and no test of the leaf itself would catch it.
