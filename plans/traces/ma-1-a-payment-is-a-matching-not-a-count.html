A Payment Is a Matching, Not a Count

MA-1 changes how the engine answers one question, asked at every priority point for every card and ability: can this player pay this mana cost right now? The greedy count it replaced added up sources. The inventory that answers now asks whether each mana can be sent to one pip, which is a matching problem with a closed-form test. Six boards walk the module, each one a finding of the phase.

mana/ma-1-exact-checkd535d2eCR tmnt2026-10-06, at review Authority: plans/mana-architecture.md §3 and §10; the review's findings, plans/handoffs/ma-1-review.md. Code: mtgsim/src/oracle/mana_supply.rs. Tests: its own module, and mtgsim/tests/phase_ma1_integration_test.rs.

The map

Where it sits. A priority question lists what the player may do. candidate_priority_actions asks each card in hand and each activated ability whether it could be begun and completed. The cheap refusals come first: timing, targets, the costs that are not mana. Only a candidate that survives them reaches the mana. The pool alone is tried first; past that, the player's inventory is read, once per priority point, the first time anything needs it, and every later candidate there shares it. If the action is chosen, CR 601.2g's window opens, and ManaAbilityWindowOffer offers the mana abilities that check counted.

Nine words to read the rest by.

inventory
ManaSupply: everything the player can make mana from at this moment: the pool, each permanent's mana abilities, the sacrifice outlets and the pool multipliers.
entry
SupplyEntry: one thing a payment can use once. The pool, or one permanent, whose mana abilities share its {T} (or the permanent itself). Everywhere's five abilities are one entry.
way
EntryWay: one alternative use of an entry and the mana it makes, after the board has acted on it. Everywhere has five ways, {W} to {G}.
demand
ManaDemand: the cost as numbers: colored pips by type, and generic.
free mana
An entry whose every way is one mana is just "one mana of any type in this set". These are pooled by type set, and are the easy part.
choice
An entry with ways of several mana (Mana Reflection doubling a land: {W}{W} or {U}{U}), or of different sizes. Its mana cannot be split across types, so the check tries its ways.
Hall's condition
The test that decides, for free mana, whether a payment exists. Enough mana in total, and for every set of the cost's colored pip kinds, at least as much mana that can pay one of them as there are pips.
pool multiplier
PoolMultiplier: a mana ability that adds the unspent pool again: Doubling Cube. It turns into a smaller demand on the mana made before it.
reserved
ReservedByCosts: what the action's own non-mana costs keep for themselves: its {T}, its sacrifices. CR 601.2h pays them after the window, so their permanents cannot also make mana.

Trace B — a Tundra and a Plains: what Hall's condition checks

A payment sends each mana to one pip. A pip of a color takes mana whose type set includes it, and generic takes any mana. Gale's theorem, Hall's with counts, says a full payment exists exactly when two things hold. There is enough mana in total. And for every set of the cost's colored pip kinds, the pips of those kinds number no more than the mana that can pay at least one of them. A shortage always shows up as some set of kinds that more pips want than mana can reach; the theorem says that is the only way one can. So the check reads the sets and never builds the payment.

  1. ManaSupply::readboard two entries: Tundra: {W} | {U}, Plains: {W}.
  2. splitmath both are one mana a way, so both are free mana: 1 of W/U, 1 of W.
  3. feasible · {W}{U}math total 2 of 2. The sets of the cost's kinds: {W, U} asks 2 and 2 can reach it; {U} asks 1 and the Tundra reaches it; {W} asks 1 and both reach it. Every set holds. Offered.
  4. feasible · {U}{U}math total 2 of 2, but {U} asks 2 and only the Tundra can pay a blue pip. Fails, so not offered. A count that only checked the total would have offered it (mutation M1 below).
Why sets, and how many. A cost with k distinct colored pip kinds has 2k − 1 nonempty sets, and k is 1 to 3 on nearly every printed card: at most seven comparisons, about 100 instructions a check. Generic pips are in the total only, because any mana pays them once the colored pips are matched.

Trace C — mana whose type is one choice: Mana Reflection on Everywhere

Mana Reflection: "If you tap a permanent for mana, it produces twice as much of that mana instead." Everywhere under it makes {W}{W} or {U}{U}, never one of each, so its mana cannot be free mana: both mana go to the type the tap chose. Sol Ring as one row of {C} and one of {G}: {C}{C} hidden, {C}{G} offered. Merging a choice into free mana of its types, "two mana, W to G", offers {W}{U} too (mutation M3).
Why the search stays small. Identical choice entries are one group with a count, so twenty Everywheres under Reflection are not 520 leaves. When every way of a group makes as much, a copy past what one asked type's pips need pays only generic, as the group's last way does, so useful_copies stops there. With a Tundra beside the Everywhere, {2}{W}{W}{U} took 6 leaves on the probe. The search stops at 4,096 leaves and answers yes; theme D of the review is about that cap.

Trace D — what a tap makes: Wild Growth on a Badlands, under Mana Reflection

A tap's production is not what the card prints once the board changes it. CR 106.6a and 106.12b replacement effects rewrite it (Mana Reflection, Deep Water, Pale Moon), and CR 605.1b triggered mana abilities add to it (Wild Growth: "Whenever enchanted land is tapped for mana, its controller adds an additional {G}"). The inventory has to read both, or it errs both ways.

  1. mana_production_watchersmemo scanned once per layer epoch, for every player at once: a replacement watcher (Mana Reflection) and a triggered mana watcher (Wild Growth). A memo hit is checked against a fresh scan in debug builds.
  2. mana_after_replacements · the Badlands' tap for {B}board the pipeline's own applicability test (applies_to_mana_production) says Reflection applies: {B} becomes {B}{B}.
  3. mana_trigger_adds · Wild Growthboard its event arm matches a tap of its host, so it adds {G}. Run through the replacements as its own production, which was not a tap for mana (CR 106.12; Mana Reflection's ruling), so it is not doubled.
  4. mana_made_bymath the entry: Badlands: {B}{B}{G} | {R}{R}{G}, a choice group of one.
  5. ChoiceSearchmath {B}{B}{G}: one leaf, offered. {B}{G}{G}: two leaves, both fail. The trigger's green is one, and the land's mana is two of one type. Not offered.
Order is the player's. Two replacement effects on one production apply in the order the player chooses (CR 616.1), each once (614.5), and an optional one may be declined. fold_mana_rewrites tries every order and keeps what no other order beats: Deep Water and Pale Moon on one tap make {U} or {C}. Six effects on one tap are up to 26·6! = 46,080 orders. That is the cap, and theme D's defect. Folding by state rather than by order makes it at most 64 states, times the few distinct mana each can hold.

What MA-1 changed, and what is still open

Every offer on the six boards is now exactly what a payment can make. Before, there was a count that treated each printed type as a separate mana. On the budget's board the rewinds that count caused went from 22.4 a game to 0.0, and the casts and activations it hid are offered. Pooled with Ironworks and the Cube, "Actions reversed" reads 2.3. Every one of the 64 reversals read in traces is a payment the random agent did not make, mostly the Cube's route on Blood Moon boards. None is an offer that no payment covers (codebase-state.md item 214).

Open, from the review (plans/handoffs/ma-1-review.md):

  • Theme C: mana_ability_cost_of reads four whole cost lists, and every other cost falls to NotCounted. That is 116 printed cards with costs the engine can already represent: life, counters, {T} with another sacrifice, {Q}.
  • Theme D: the fold's 46,080 orders at its cap, and the search's lean-yes past 4,096 leaves.
  • Item 213: the shapes the inventory reads only one way.

Check yourself

Believing you understand the module and understanding it differ in one way you can test: whether you can predict what it does on a board you have not seen, and say what breaks without each piece. Three kinds of question follow. Answer each before you open it. Every answer below came from the engine at this commit.

3. Say why

Why is Everywhere one entry and not five?

Its five abilities share one {T}, and a payment can use a {T} once. In one 601.2g window only a mana ability could untap it again. No printed mana ability untaps another permanent, and the one that untaps at all, Pili-Pala, untaps itself as its {Q} cost. Five entries would count five mana (trace A, mutation M2).

Why are a choice entry's ways tried one at a time, rather than its mana added to the free table?

Because its mana is not independent: both of Reflection's mana go to the one type the tap made. Free mana of W to G would let one go to white and one to blue, which no tap makes (trace C, mutation M3). Hall's condition is exact only for mana whose type is its own choice, so the check enumerates what is not and runs Hall at each leaf.

Why does the pool-multiplier transform round up, and add the input after?

Every mana left after the input becomes f of its own type, so n pips of a type need ⌈n/f⌉ of it left: one mana left over cannot become half a pip. The input is paid first, from what was made before, so it is added back on top. Rounding down offers what cannot be paid (mutation M6, caught by the property test).

Why is the action's own {T} reserved before the split, not after?

The split decides what mana exists. A permanent tapped for mana in the window (601.2g) cannot then pay the {T} at 601.2h, so its tap is not mana at all for this payment (trace E, mutation M4).

Why is the inventory read at most once per priority point, and only when a candidate needs it?

It costs about 29,000 instructions, and most priority points never reach the mana. The cheaper refusals come first, so the design counted 17,906 inventories against 58,921 priority points. Read inside each check, it would be one per card; read eagerly, about 41,000 more. That is why candidate_priority_actions shares one lazily filled cell (the review's theme B is about its shape).

What is the property test's blind spot, and what covers it?

It builds entries directly, from random shapes, so it proves Hall's condition, the choice search and the transform against brute force, but never ManaSupply::read: how a card becomes an entry, what a cost reserves, what a sacrifice outlet counts, what the watchers change. Those are covered by one test per card shape (mutations M2, M4, M5, M7). The review's theme C, widening the cost reader, has to add to them.
