A Trigger Is Matched at the Close

TR-1 is the first phase in which the engine reads its own performed events back: a triggered ability is matched against the records of one batch, at the moment that batch's last rider has run and before anyone receives priority. The claim the page walks is the one the design argued for and the tests assert — the window is the event — on the three boards that make it bite: two permanents entering together, an artifact dying with the creatures it watches, and a mana trigger that never reaches the stack.

triggers/tr-1CR tmnt2026-09-19, at close Authority for the design: plans/triggers-architecture.md §2, §4.1–§4.4, §4.7, §5; the archive's TR-1 notes. Tests: mtgsim/tests/phase_tr1_integration_test.rs.

The map

Three boards, each about one property of the split between detection and placement. The first is CR 603.6a's sentence made observable — "all permanents on the battlefield (including the newcomers) are checked" — with Humility as the newcomer that decides whether the other newcomer has an ability at all. The second is CR 603.10a: an artifact that is in the graveyard by the time anything is checked, and still triggers twice. The third is CR 605.4a: a trigger that resolves inside the dispatch and never becomes a pending entry.

Trace A — Humility and a creature with an ETB enter as one event

Player 0 holds "Arriving Scholar" (a 1/1 with "When this enters, draw a card"), player 1 holds Humility, and both are put onto the battlefield by one execute_actions of two EnterBattlefield proposals — the shape a spell that puts two permanents onto the battlefield would produce. The question is whether the Scholar's ability is read before or after Humility is on the board.

  1. execute_actionsstate opens the batch: mark = events.len(), window = Some(BatchId(n)). Nothing is matched yet, and nothing will be until the close.
  2. perform_action(EnterBattlefield · Scholar)board moves the card, builds the entity, register_static_effects files the Scholar in trigger_sources (a printed triggered ability that functions on the battlefield), and place_on_battlefield emits PermanentEnteredBattlefield — stamped with the batch, so emit_record does not dispatch it.
  3. perform_action(EnterBattlefield · Humility)board the same, plus Humility's static ability registers its Layer 4/6/7b rows and bumps the layer epoch. The Scholar's memoized frame, if any, is stale from here.
  4. dispatch_after_batchstate batch_depth is back to 0: this was the outermost batch, so the window is dispatched — the two entry records, in order.
  5. dispatch_inner · the gatestate trigger_sources holds the Scholar (and Soul Warden, on the sibling board), so the gate passes. On the pools as they were before this phase every set is empty and this is where every dispatch returned.
  6. find_matches · candidatesboard battlefield_ids_ordered() filtered by trigger_sources: the Scholar. For each candidate, compute_characteristics — the effective list, walked at the epoch Humility's rows bumped. The Scholar's frame comes back with no abilities.
  7. find_matches · the loopboard for each record, for each candidate, for each Effect::Triggered on its list — and the list is empty. No trigger record is written for an ability that does not exist; nothing is queued.
  8. dispatch_innerstate matches.is_empty() → return. pending_triggers is empty; the test's assertion.
Why per record would have been wrong. Had the matcher run inside emit_record at step 2, the Scholar's list would have been read before Humility's rows existed: one trigger queued, which CR 603.6a and CR 603.6b together say should never have triggered — "the permanent is never on the battlefield with its unmodified characteristics", and every permanent is checked after "an event puts one or more permanents onto the battlefield". Soul Warden beside two bear tokens (CreateTokens with three defs) is the same reading from the other side: three entries, one window, two triggers — one per other creature, Soul Warden's own entry refused by EachOther in the same loop.

Trace B — an artifact dying in the wipe that kills the creatures it watches

"Mourning Idol", an artifact with "Whenever a creature dies, you gain 1 life", beside two vanilla creatures; a Sol Ring stands in for the spell and execute_actions carries three Destroy proposals. ATOM-603.10a-001's board, and Blood Artist's ruling on its own card is the same walk with a target at the end.

  1. execute_actionsstate one batch, three members; each Destroy performer nests a ZoneChange that joins the batch (CR 120.3f's rule, generalized).
  2. perform_zone_change · eachframe before move_object, the CR 603.10a frame: compute_characteristics_uncached, boxed onto the record as lki. The Idol's frame carries its triggered ability; the creatures' carry Creature.
  3. cleanup_zone_state · the Idolstate removes it from trigger_sources — the set says nothing about it from here. A gate built on sets alone would now be empty.
  4. dispatch_inner · the gatelog the sixth probe: any_frame_source scans each window record's lki for a triggered def, and the Idol's frame has one. The gate passes with every set empty.
  5. find_matches · framesframe leg 2: for every ZoneChange with lki: Some, a candidate whose id is the departed object, whose controller is the frame's, whose ability list is the frame's, and whose frame is set — three of them, the Idol among them.
  6. match_def · the Idol's def against record 1frame a frame candidate is asked look-back arms only, and ZoneChange { from: Some(Battlefield) } looks back; the visibility check is skipped (it was a permanent). The arm's predicates: from and to match, and subject_matches(Filter(Creature), creature A) reads the record's frame, since the creature is a graveyard card now.
  7. match_def · against record 0 (the Idol's own death)frame Filter(Creature) against the Idol's own frame: an artifact. Refused as condition; the trigger record says so.
  8. find_matches · queuestate two Matches, PerOccurrence, each with one record and the creature's ObjectRef; two PendingTriggers under player 0 with source_card the Idol's, held because the Idol will not be in the store's battlefield at placement.
  9. place_pending_triggersplayer two entries, one controller, identical abilities — but the bindings differ (two records, two subjects), so the elision's expiry condition applies and OrderTriggers is asked. The stack object is built from source_card, which is why a source in a graveyard, or gone from the game, can still put an ability on the stack (CR 113.7a).
Guile's boards are the negative. "From anywhere" is from: None, not on CR 603.10's list, so a frame candidate is never asked it; the card is read where it is after the move, off the zone map arrive_in_zone filled because CR 113.6k puts the ability's function everywhere. Humility on the battlefield changes nothing (the ability is back in the graveyard); Yixlid Jailer strips it there and the trigger never fires — the seventh ruling, both halves.

Trace C — Wild Growth inside the mana window

A Forest wearing Wild Growth, tapped for {G} through activate_mana_ability — the same call the CR 601.2g window makes while a spell's cost is being paid.

  1. activate_mana_ability → resolve_mana_effectboard proposes ProduceMana { source: Forest, tapped_for_mana: true } through execute_action; the performer adds {G} and emits ManaAdded, stamped with the batch.
  2. dispatch_after_batch → find_matchesboard Wild Growth is in trigger_sources; its arm is ManaAdded { source: Host, tapped_for_mana: Some(true) }, and Subject::Host is the candidate's attached_to — the Forest. Matched.
  3. dispatch_inner · is_mana_ability(def)state derived, not tagged: no instance of "target", every arm ManaAdded, a ProduceMana in the effect (CR 605.1b's three criteria). The trigger record says mana: true.
  4. resolve_triggered_manaboard "its controller adds": the Host recipient is the Forest's controller. A ProduceMana { source: Wild Growth, tapped_for_mana: false } is proposed through the chokepoint — a new outermost batch, since the first has closed — so a replacement or a "can't" sees it like any other production.
  5. dispatch_after_batch · the second windowboard Wild Growth is asked about its own record: tapped_for_mana is false, the source is the Aura and not the host. Refused as condition. This is Wild Growth's ruling — "the additional mana is not an ability of the land" — and why two Wild Growths on one Forest add two and not four.
  6. activate_mana_abilitystate returns with {G}{G} in the pool, pending_triggers empty and the stack untouched: CR 605.4a's "immediately after the mana ability that triggered it, without waiting for priority", inside the window that asked for the mana.

What TR-1 changed, trace by trace — and what is still open

Trace A is the design's §4.1 refinement in one board: a per-record matcher at emit_event would have queued the Scholar's ability, and the batch close is what lets CR 603.6b apply before CR 603.6a is asked. The cost is the window's bookkeeping — a mark and a batch id at each of the three batch doors — and the reward is that "one or more" (CR 603.2c) needs no second pass. Trace B is where the gate turned out to need a sixth probe: a departed permanent is out of every set by the time its window closes, so the frames themselves are the gate for leg 2, and the archive's note 4 records the cost as the A/B measured it (inside the budget). What Trace B does not show, and main item 167 records, is a look-back arm on a permanent that survives the batch: it reads the list it has after the event, where CR 603.10 wants the one before. Trace C is the one path that resolves inside a dispatch, and it is entered from the dispatcher rather than being a second resolver: the same ProduceMana proposal an activated mana ability makes, with the Aura as the source and tapped_for_mana: false, which is what keeps the second window from re-triggering the first.

Open, and named in codebase-state.md's TR-1 section: item 167 above; the intervening "if" reading "you" off a source that has left (169, TR-2's evaluator); a frame candidate's identity epoch (168, TR-4's frame); and the two arms that shipped narrow so §13's row could close (171).
